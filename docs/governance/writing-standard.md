# Writing Standard

Status: canonical.

Purpose: hold the one definition of fidelity, clarity and elegance that every text of this
repository meant for a person answers to.

See also: [Product Design
Contract](product-design-contract.md#evidence-planes-and-completion-claims) and
[Docs Registry](docs-registry.md).

## The Definition

_Fidelity, clarity, elegance (信、达、雅)_: **fidelity is the root; clarity comes before elegance.** From
Yan Fu's translator's notes (译例言) to _Evolution and Ethics_ (1898), they apply to all writing meant
for a person to read, in Chinese or English: documents, reports, replies, handoffs, commit messages,
and the human-readable text of interfaces and command output. The first sentence of each item can be
cited alone.

- **Fidelity (信): meaning, facts, conditions and limits, who is responsible, and what is uncertain
  all stay intact.** The words are accurate; nothing is exaggerated or omitted, and no guess is
  written as fact.
- **Clarity (达): the reader understands the text and can judge and act on it.** It rests on the
  writer understanding the subject accurately first. The meaning arrives whole, in fluent, natural,
  idiomatic language, without word-for-word rendering of another language, piled jargon, or stock
  and templated phrasing. Clarity is the way fidelity is achieved.
- **Elegance (雅): on top of fidelity and clarity, the writing is fitting and beautiful.** Aesthetic
  judgment, taste and refinement shape the wording, the structure, the rhythm and transitions of the
  prose, and the layout to suit the subject and the reader, so that the writing carries and stays
  with the reader. Yan Fu's 尔雅 meant classical, refined diction, which he held also makes clarity
  easier; here it is taken as literary grace, extended to the aesthetics of the whole text. Elegance
  is not ornament, archaism or stacked set phrases; restraint, concision, order and consistency are
  means to it, not its definition. It is checked by a reader other than the writer finding the text
  natural and fitting.
- **When they conflict, fidelity prevails over clarity and clarity over elegance.** Keep a
  qualification rather than drop it for smoothness, and never change the meaning to make a sentence
  read better.
